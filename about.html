About LearningBoard

LearningBoard is a student-built platform where students can share what they have learned at work and help each other grow. Whether you picked up a communication tip during an internship or discovered a tech trick at a part-time job, your experience is valuable to someone else.

Who Is It For?

LearningBoard is built for students who have real work experience and want to share it, and for students who want to learn from peers before entering the workplace themselves.

  • Students in internships or co-op placements who are picking up professional skills on the job and want to document and share what they learn.
  • Students with part-time or summer jobs who have practical workplace tips that could help their peers.
  • Students preparing to enter the workforce who want a realistic preview of what working life actually looks like.
  • Students in any program — the four skill categories cover lessons that apply across every field and industry.

Our Mission

Textbooks teach theory. LearningBoard teaches what actually happens on the job. Our mission is to bridge the gap between classroom learning and real workplace experience by giving students a space to share honest, practical insights.

How It Works

  1. Share a Learning

    Post something you learned at work — a skill, a lesson, a mistake, or a win. Keep it real and useful for other students.

  2. Browse by Category

    Explore posts filtered by skill category: Communication, Tech Skills, Teamwork, or Problem Solving.

  3. Mark as Helpful

    Found a post useful? Give it a helpful vote so the best insights rise to the top for everyone.

Skill Categories

  • 💬 Communication

    Tips for presenting ideas, writing professionally, and working with clients or colleagues.

  • 💻 Tech Skills

    Practical software, tools, and coding insights picked up on the job.

  • 👥 Teamwork

    Lessons about collaboration, remote work, and building strong team relationships.

  • 🧩 Problem Solving

    Creative approaches to workplace challenges and critical thinking strategies.
